01

The growth

2015 → 2025

A structural rise, not a short-term spike.

Irish data-centre electricity consumption increased from 1,240 GWh in 2015 to 7,662 GWh in 2025 using the sum of quarterly CSO observations.

That is a compound annual growth rate of roughly 20%. Growth accelerated sharply around 2020–2022, then moderated — but consumption still reached a record level in 2025.

The key signal

The rate of growth has slowed, but the level of demand keeps climbing.

A changing demand profile

From one unit in twenty to almost one in four.

Data centres accounted for about 5.0% of metered electricity in 2015. By 2025, that share had risen to 23.2%.

23.2% of metered electricity in 2025

“Metered electricity” is the CSO measure used here; it is not identical to total grid supply.

03

Who drove the increase?

Net metered-demand growth

Most of the decade's increase came from data centres.

Between 2015 and 2025, data-centre consumption increased by roughly 6.42 TWh. That represents about 76.6% of the net increase in total metered electricity consumption over the same period.

This does not mean other demand was static — it means the data-centre contribution dominated the net growth.

Demand is only half the story

The grid has to expand and decarbonise at the same time.

In 2025, gas remained the largest domestic generation source, wind provided a major share of generation, solar was growing quickly, and net imports were material to total supply.

Is the projection outside the planning range?

No. It sits between EirGrid's Median and High growth scenarios.

The independent statistical projection reaches an index of roughly 205 by 2035, compared with EirGrid's 195 Median and 228 High scenarios.

Important scope difference

EirGrid uses MVA for a broader “Data Centre and New Technology Load” category. CSO measures metered data-centre energy consumption in GWh. We compare growth rates, not raw units.

10

What it means

The takeaway

The question is not simply whether data-centre demand grows.

The system impact depends on whether generation, transmission, interconnection, storage, flexibility and decarbonisation expand alongside it.

Demand

Growth is already structural.

Data centres now represent a materially larger share of metered electricity than a decade ago.

Adequacy

Energy volume is only one constraint.

Average MW, peak conditions, connection capacity and firm supply all matter.

Decarbonisation

Cleaner electricity changes the carbon outcome.

The same demand can have very different emissions consequences under different grid intensities.
